Meet the plaque

Tiny critters. Big appetites.

Plaque is a sticky film of germs that grows on teeth every single day. You can't see the germs, so the book gives them faces, hair and very bad manners.

Here's how they cause trouble:

1. They eatGerms gobble up the sugar left on your teeth.
2. They poopThen they leave acid behind. Yes, really.
3. Teeth hurtThat acid makes soft spots that turn into cavities.

Grab your toothbrush and a pea-sized blob of fluoride toothpaste.

Brush twice a day

Morning and bedtime, for two minutes each. Little kids need a grown-up to help until they can tie their own shoes.

Floss where teeth touch

A toothbrush can't reach between teeth. Floss once a day to evict the critters hiding there.

Starve the plaque

Save sweets and juice for mealtimes and drink water between meals. Less sugar means fewer plaque snacks.

Visit the dentist

Go by your first birthday, then about every six months. Dentists are plaque detectives.

Spit, don't rinse

After brushing, spit out the extra toothpaste but skip the big rinse. The fluoride keeps working.

Use the right amount

Under age 3, a smear the size of a grain of rice. Ages 3 to 6, a blob the size of a pea.
